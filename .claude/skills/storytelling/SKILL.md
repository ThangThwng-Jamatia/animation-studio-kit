# Storytelling: the psychology layer

Load this **before scripting or planning any video**. The format skill (dark-fable-2d, build-it-3d, 3d-facts, video-editor…) still owns visuals and pipeline; this skill owns the *story*. Every script must pass the **Story Gate** at the bottom before "go".

**Dark Fable shorts, Build It 3D and 3D Facts:** also read `references/viral-shorts-standard.md` (measured 2026-11-09 against the viral inspo) and pass `_engine/scripts/shorts_gate.py script`. For those formats it refines three rules here:
- the protagonist is one specific person named by **role** ("the cashier", "a con man"), not by name and date;
- the ending is the **payoff line only**, with no lesson and no share prompt;
- sentences are long and joined with so / but / you see (12–20 words), not short punches.

## 1. What the research says (why it works)

| Finding | What it means for us |
|---|---|
| **Curiosity = an information gap** (Loewenstein, 1994; Recht & Yeung, Oxford). Curiosity peaks when the gap is *small but real*, and dies if the answer comes too late. | Open a specific question in the first second; answer it, but only after opening the next one. Vague mystery ("you won't believe…") is no gap at all. |
| **Tension makes people feel what the character feels** (Paul Zak: character stories with rising tension raise cortisol, attention, then oxytocin, and that predicts action). | No tension = no empathy. Every story needs a person wanting something and something in the way. |
| **Neural coupling** (Hasson, Princeton): a listener's brain activity syncs with the storyteller's when following a story, and the better the sync, the better the understanding. | One clear thread at a time. Confusion breaks the sync and people swipe. |
| **Narrative transportation** (Green & Brock): when people are "lost in a story" they argue back less and remember more. | Teach *inside* a story, not next to it. Facts stated as lectures get skipped; facts the character pays for stick. |
| **Identifiable-victim effect**: one named person moves us more than statistics about millions. | One protagonist, one face, concrete details (a cracked phone, 2:47 a.m.), not "people" or "many". |
| **Prediction error / dopamine**: the brain rewards events that beat its prediction. | Pattern interrupts and twists every few beats. If the viewer can predict the next line, rewrite it. |
| **Zeigarnik (unfinished things stay in mind)** is weaker in studies than blogs claim, but open loops still work as a *promise*. | Use open loops sparingly and **always pay them off**. Broken promises teach the viewer to stop trusting the hook. |
| **Peak-end rule** (Kahneman): people judge an experience by its peak and its final moment. | Build one clear emotional peak and end on a strong last beat. The ending decides shares, rewatches and follows. |
| **Self-relevance**: the brain prioritises anything about *me*. | Second person ("you"), ordinary-life openings, problems the viewer has really had. |

Platform data (vendor blogs, directional only): most drop-off happens in the **first ~3 s**; after that the curve flattens. So the hook carries most of the weight, then the middle needs a re-hook every few seconds.

## 2. The hook (0–3 s): four jobs in one line + one image

1. **Pattern interrupt**: motion or an odd image on frame 1. Never a logo, a title or a slow fade.
2. **Self-relevance**: "you", or an everyman in a situation the viewer recognises.
3. **A specific gap**: a concrete question the viewer now *needs* answered.
4. **Stakes**: what is lost if it goes wrong.

Hook patterns (pick one and write 3 versions, keep the best):
- **In medias res**: start at the worst moment. "Day 3. The water's gone."
- **Contradiction**: "The safest thing you can do in a fire is *not* run."
- **Specific number + consequence**: "You have 4 minutes before the cold kills you."
- **The 'wrong way'**: show the mistake everyone makes, then "here's why that kills you".
- **Ending first**: show the payoff (the finished build, the defeated demon), then "how did he get here?"
- **Direct challenge**: "Pause and guess what he does next."

Visual hook = spoken hook. The first frame must *show* the gap, not just say it (works with sound off, captions mid-frame per feed-look).

## 3. Structure: the spine

Use **"but / therefore" instead of "and then"** (Parker & Stone). Each beat is caused by the previous one and complicates the next one.

**Shorts (30–75 s)**
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–3 s | Hook: gap + stakes |
| 3–10 s | Context in one line: who, where, what they want |
| 10–45 s | Escalation: 3 obstacles, each worse (rule of three). Re-hook every 5–8 s: new question, new reveal, new visual |
| ~70% | **Peak / twist**: the moment that beats the prediction |
| last 5 s | Payoff + lasting line (a lesson, a twist or a cliffhanger). Final frame loops cleanly into frame 1 when possible |

**Longs (8–20 min)**: cold open (the peak, out of context) → promise ("by the end you'll know…") → chapters, each a mini-story with its own gap and payoff → midpoint reversal → climax → short resolution + a reason to watch the next video. Re-hook at every chapter card; never let 60 s pass without a new question.

**Series (Day Series, Ep parts)**: each episode answers last episode's cliffhanger in the first 5 s, then opens a bigger one. Escalate stakes every episode.

## 4. Connection: make them care

- **Want + wound**: in one line, the protagonist wants something concrete and has a fear or flaw. ("He wants to quit his phone. He's tried 11 times.")
- **Show the viewer themself**: the everyman does the thing the viewer does (scrolls at 2 a.m., snoozes the alarm). Recognition comes before empathy.
- **Vulnerability before victory**: failure on screen before success. A win is only felt if a loss came first.
- **Sensory specifics**: one concrete detail per scene (sound, texture, time). Details make it feel real.
- **Inner voice**: give the character a thought the viewer has had but never said.
- **Choice**: the climax is a *decision* the character makes, not luck. The viewer rehearses the choice.
- **Earned emotion**: music and slow-mo amplify a feeling the story has already built, never replace it.

## 5. Retention mechanics (every edit)

- **Re-hook cadence**: a new gap, reveal or visual change every 5–8 s (shorts) or 30–60 s (longs).
- **Nested loops**: open loop B before closing loop A. Never close everything at once until the end.
- **Cut the throat-clearing**: no intros, "hey guys", recaps or setup that doesn't raise a question. Delete the first line of every draft and see if it's better.
- **Contrast**: alternate tension/release, loud/quiet, fast/slow. Flat energy kills watch time faster than slow energy.
- **Payoff every promise** made in the hook, on screen and clearly.
- **Ending**: no "thanks for watching". End on the peak line, a twist or a cliffhanger; for shorts, a loop.

## 6. Ethics

Hook with real stakes and real facts; deliver what the title promises. Clickbait that doesn't pay off raises swipes and kills the channel's trust. Survival and health facts must be true (day-series already requires this).

## 7. Story Gate (check before "go")

Write the answers in the plan. If any is "no", rewrite before building.

1. Can the hook be said in one sentence, and does frame 1 *show* it?
2. Is there one protagonist with a concrete want and a clear obstacle?
3. Are the stakes clear by 5 s?
4. Is every beat linked by *but/therefore*?
5. Is there a re-hook at least every 8 s (shorts) / 60 s (longs)?
6. Is there one peak/twist the viewer would not predict?
7. Is every open loop paid off?
8. Does the last line land (lesson, twist, cliffhanger or loop), with no outro?
9. Would the viewer see themself in it?
10. Read the script aloud at speed: any line where you'd swipe? Cut it.
